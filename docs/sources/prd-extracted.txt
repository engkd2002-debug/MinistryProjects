0. How to use this document
Build contract: P0 requirements are mandatory for an initial trustworthy release; P1 requirements complete the core research intelligence experience; P2 items are later opportunities.
Source labels: [H] means implemented or explicitly described by the supplied HTML prototype; [P] means strategic intent shown in the supplied presentation; [N] means a new product requirement based on the user’s brief or a design recommendation. The prototype is the primary source.
Acceptance first: every requirement has observable acceptance criteria. AI implementation tools must not replace these with aesthetically impressive but nonfunctional screens.
Safety of interpretation: demonstration records, simulated rankings, local-storage users, and visual gaps must never be presented as verified national statistics, formal accreditation, or investment recommendations.
Open decisions: unresolved policy and institutional decisions appear in Section 19. Builders should use configurable defaults and flag dependencies instead of inventing facts.
1. Executive product brief
1.1 Problem
Syrian scientific work is distributed across universities, postgraduate programs, hospitals, research centers, laboratories, institutes, and other institutions. Records vary in availability, format, completeness, classification and review status. A researcher cannot reliably discover prior work or compare related studies; a steward cannot see the quality of coverage; and decision-makers cannot distinguish a genuinely underserved research area from missing or unreported data. [P]
1.2 Product vision
Build a national, bilingual, interactive discovery and research-intelligence platform that makes research findable, comparable, traceable and governable. Users should be able to move seamlessly between three mutually synchronized workspaces: Geographic Explorer (where activity appears and where coverage is weak), Research Workspace (search, review, compare and operate), and Insights Dashboard (evidence for decisions). A governed content pipeline powers all three. [H/P/N]
1.3 Outcome statement
A university researcher finds comparable work before starting a project; an institutional editor contributes structured records without corrupting the catalog; reviewers validate provenance and metadata; administrators understand coverage; decision-makers identify research distribution and candidate investment opportunities with explicit uncertainty.
1.4 Product principles
Evidence before spectacle: every number, region shade and insight is traceable to the underlying records, filters, update time and coverage status.
One query, three perspectives: map, results and dashboard share a single canonical filter/search state; changing one view updates the others.
Geography is a lens, not the database: the same study can have an origin institution, contributors, multiple affiliations, fieldwork locations and research subjects; none should be conflated.
No false absences: a zero count means “no records in the current dataset and filter scope,” not automatically “no research exists.”
Accessible Arabic by default: right-to-left is a first-class design system, with semantic charts, keyboard-compatible mapping, readable numerals and English switching.
Human accountability over AI automation: AI can suggest matches, summaries and anomalies but cannot independently certify research, delete records or publish without appropriate authorized review.

5.2 Cross-cutting shell

6. Canonical navigation and state contracts
Canonical filter schema (initial): `q`, `governorate_ids[]`, `city_ids[]`, `institution_ids[]`, `institution_types[]`, `sector_ids[]`, `specialty_ids[]`, `branch_ids[]`, `research_types[]`, `years[]`, `year_from`, `year_to`, `author_ids[]`, `keywords[]`, `statuses[]` (authorized only), `source_quality`, `coverage_status`, `sort`, `page`. Values use stable IDs, not free-form display names. [H/N]
Synchronization: a facet/map click updates the canonical state, chips, count, results and map/dashboard; all fetch requests use the same scoped snapshot; facet options update with remaining counts and retain selected values.
Selection: selected record(s), map selection and compare tray are distinct state objects; changing filters must not silently erase comparisons. If comparisons fall outside the current scope, show why and offer remove/restore.
Deep links: loading a shared link reconstructs the same user-authorized filter/view state and reports inaccessible or no-longer-available records clearly.
All states: loading (skeleton), no results (suggest clear controls), no coverage data, errors/retry, offline/read-only and permissions denied must be designed before polished charts.
7. Geographic Explorer — requirements and UX
MAP-001 — Syrian geographic navigation  [P0]
Requirement. Show an accurate Syria governorate layer with all 14 governorates; permit hover/focus, click to select, zoom/pan/reset, and keyboard-accessible non-map equivalent. Drill down to cities, institutions and record clusters as zoom level/data permit.
UX / decision rationale. Progressive geographic disclosure prevents hundreds of overlapping pins; map alternatives serve keyboard, screen-reader and low-bandwidth users.
Acceptance criteria. Selecting a governorate filters matching records in every workspace; a user can return to the national view; keyboard list has identical selection semantics; viewport and filters can be restored by URL.
Traceability. [H/N].
MAP-002 — Institutional and sector layers  [P0]
Requirement. Allow toggles for universities, hospitals, research centers, laboratories, institutes and other registered organizations. Each institution has a verified location (or explicit “location not verified”) and a detail route. Support sector and parent/child hierarchy.
UX / decision rationale. The project spans sectors, while the HTML’s university-centric references would otherwise hide hospitals and centers. Layer toggles and a restrained legend prevent confusion.
Acceptance criteria. A public user can toggle a sector, inspect institution count and navigate to a verified institution profile; location-unverified entities appear in a directory and cannot be silently pinned to guessed coordinates.
Traceability. [N].
MAP-003 — Quantitative map encodings  [P0]
Requirement. Support proportional cluster bubbles for record counts and a choropleth/coverage layer with a metric selector. Specify metric, unit, period, publication status, denominator, coverage completeness and last refresh in the legend.
UX / decision rationale. A map’s color implies meaning; one color scale cannot simultaneously represent activity, opportunity, completeness and institutional quality.
Acceptance criteria. Bubble size correlates monotonically with count; colors have text labels; legend updates with filters and has a table alternative; no metric silently switches on zoom.
Traceability. [H/N].
MAP-004 — Geographic drill-down & detail  [P0]
Requirement. Click a governorate, city, institution or cluster to open a contextual panel: counts, top sectors/specialties, active filters, coverage status, data freshness, institution breakdown and “Open matching research.”
UX / decision rationale. The user should retain spatial context while moving from abstract geography into actionable records.
Acceptance criteria. Panel reflects shared query, supports open/close without resetting filters, has meaningful loading/empty states and a visible route to the research workspace.
Traceability. [H/N].
MAP-005 — Coverage-aware gap mode  [P1]
Requirement. Provide two clearly separated lenses: (A) observed record density and (B) evidence-qualified opportunity indicators. For governorate × domain cells, show observed count, institution coverage, reporting period, expected baseline (if approved), completeness and reason for flagging.
UX / decision rationale. The current prototype marks zero-count cells red. An ethical decision tool must distinguish under-observation from verified underinvestment.
Acceptance criteria. Every “gap” can be expanded to show numerator, denominator, coverage quality, methodology and a note “No registered records ≠ no existing research”; insufficient coverage produces an “Unknown / insufficient data” state, never a negative verdict.
Traceability. [H/N].
MAP-006 — Heatmap and collaboration networks  [P1]
Requirement. Offer optional density overlay and cross-institution collaboration edges. Edges use confirmed shared researcher identities or explicit co-authorship/institution relationships, not text-name coincidence; provide a ranked table alternative.
UX / decision rationale. These views reveal clusters and connected work but become misleading if name disambiguation is weak.
Acceptance criteria. Users can switch overlays independently; edge tooltip explains evidence and count; ambiguous identities are excluded/flagged pending resolution; map remains navigable when overlays are off.
Traceability. [H/N].
MAP-007 — Temporal exploration  [P1]
Requirement. Filter multiple years or a continuous range and optionally animate year-by-year changes with a pause/step alternative. Default to a static range; respect reduced motion.
UX / decision rationale. Temporal change is a critical analytical question but autoplay can obscure comparisons and harm accessibility.
Acceptance criteria. Year selection updates shared data; an accessible table shows equivalent yearly values; no fabricated interpolated years.
Traceability. [H/N].
8. Research Workspace — discovery and analysis
RES-001 — Search and linked filters  [P0]
Requirement. Provide prominent full-text search of titles, abstracts, keywords, author, institution and subject, plus hierarchical and multi-select facets for geography, institution type, institution, field, branch, research type, year and publication/source status. Normalize Arabic orthographic variants safely.
UX / decision rationale. Most users arrive with a known topic rather than a map coordinate. Faceted search combines immediate discovery with transparent control.
Acceptance criteria. A query changes result count and map/dashboard consistently; filter chips can be removed independently; Arabic diacritics are handled; filters have keyboard and clear-all support; no unpublished records leak to the public.
Traceability. [H/N].
RES-002 — Explainable assisted search  [P1]
Requirement. Translate natural-language questions into editable structured chips (e.g., “AI theses in Aleppo after 2023”) and ranked results with match explanations. Start with audited synonym/keyword logic and later permit semantic embeddings only behind evaluation and confidence controls.
UX / decision rationale. The prototype’s transparent keyword matching is a safe baseline; generative/embedding suggestions must not silently override precise filters.
Acceptance criteria. User sees extracted topic, region, institution, year and type; can remove/change each; explanation distinguishes exact filters, synonyms and semantic similarity; system can fall back to keyword mode.
Traceability. [H/N].
RES-003 — Results information hierarchy  [P0]
Requirement. Show a compact result card/table with localized title, authors, year, research type, institution(s), location, matched terms, source availability, status (authorized users), and stable ID. Include relevant sorting and pagination or virtualized results.
UX / decision rationale. Researchers scan titles and evidence before administrative metadata. Persistent compare action is more useful than multiple decorative tags.
Acceptance criteria. Cards expose title and provenance without opening; selecting a card opens detail; sort/filter and scroll position survive return; public users see only published status.
Traceability. [H/N].
RES-004 — Record detail and source provenance  [P0]
Requirement. Dedicated detail page and contextual preview show bilingual titles/abstracts where provided; authors with roles; all organizations; academic taxonomy; research and fieldwork locations separately; dates; type; keywords; identifiers; source URL or licensed file; review/publication metadata; related research.
UX / decision rationale. A “research record” is a citable object, not just a card. It must distinguish metadata registration from peer-reviewed scientific validity.
Acceptance criteria. Every published record shows stable URL, citation-ready fields, source link status, verification statement, last updated timestamp and “Report correction”; missing fields say “Not supplied.”
Traceability. [H/N].
RES-005 — Compare 2–8 records  [P1]
Requirement. Support selection of two to eight studies into a persistent comparison tray. Compare institution(s), specialty, methodology where available, year, type, authors, keywords, abstract and source/quality indicators; visually mark differences with text, not color alone.
UX / decision rationale. The HTML comparison tray is a strong decision aid. A matrix makes similarities explicit before beginning a potentially duplicative study.
Acceptance criteria. Compare is disabled for fewer than two; every differing row has a visible marker; long abstracts can collapse; comparison respects permissions and remains available via shareable scoped link.
Traceability. [H].
RES-006 — Related research and topic exploration  [P1]
Requirement. Recommend related published records using explained connections (same specialty, keywords, cited authors, verified associations). Provide “why shown” and ability to exclude an irrelevant match.
UX / decision rationale. Researchers should understand recommendation logic and avoid treating similarity scores as claims of originality.
Acceptance criteria. Every suggestion has visible evidence of relation; never displays drafts to unauthorized users; empty related results are explained.
Traceability. [H/N].
RES-007 — Institutional and specialty profiles  [P1]
Requirement. Create drill-down pages with the institutional identity, type, locations, parent system, research volumes, coverage flags, people, leading fields, recent works, completeness and collaboration partners. Provide equivalent profiles for specialties.
UX / decision rationale. Institution and topic profiles connect map, operations and analytics without trapping users inside a modal.
Acceptance criteria. Profile totals equal matching published records under current filters; users can navigate from record, map and dashboard; low coverage is visible; no unsupported rankings or performance labels.
Traceability. [H/N].
RES-008 — Saved views and export  [P1]
Requirement. Support shareable filters and exports to CSV, XLSX and JSON, with permissions, selected columns, localized headings, filters, export date and provenance. Larger asynchronous exports require a job/status UI.
UX / decision rationale. Research analysis often continues outside the platform; export must preserve the meaning of a scoped query.
Acceptance criteria. Exported rows equal authorized query (not just visible page); export excludes confidential fields; metadata includes applied scope, timestamp and schema version; CSV injection values are neutralized.
Traceability. [H/N].
9. Insights Dashboard — actionable intelligence
INS-001 — Decision-ready overview  [P0]
Requirement. Display contextual KPI cards for verified published research records, contributing institutions, verified researchers (where deduplicated), covered governorates, period and latest update. Distinguish raw count from coverage-adjusted measures.
UX / decision rationale. The prototype exposes counts; production requires explicit denominators and time/status labels to avoid overclaiming.
Acceptance criteria. Each metric has definition, applied filters, data freshness, click-through drill-down and a no-data state; numbers reconcile with server aggregates for the same query.
Traceability. [H/N].
INS-002 — Distribution and growth  [P0]
Requirement. Show records by year, governorate, institution, sector, specialty, branch and type; include a sortable accompanying data table and click-to-filter interaction.
UX / decision rationale. Multiple distributions reveal differences between volume, geography and subject concentration without requiring complex visuals.
Acceptance criteria. Every chart uses labeled axes/units; supports keyboard selection and table; clicking an item adjusts shared state; small sample/no coverage has an explicit warning.
Traceability. [H/N].
INS-003 — Gap / opportunity matrix  [P1]
Requirement. Show governorate × discipline and institution-type × discipline matrices with cells for records, reporting institutions, coverage percent and gaps only when methodology conditions are satisfied. Allow comparison with previous period and configurable, approved baselines.
UX / decision rationale. A zero is not a measure of societal need. Separate metadata coverage risk from actual research opportunity.
Acceptance criteria. Cells expose a method tooltip and evidence panel; unqualified cells read “Insufficient data”; configurable thresholds are versioned and documented; stakeholder can export the evidence behind each flag.
Traceability. [H/N].
INS-004 — Quality and pipeline health  [P0]
Requirement. Private dashboard displays completeness, validation exceptions, duplicate candidates, ingestion errors, submission-to-publication funnel, queue age, time-to-decision and institution reporting cadence.
UX / decision rationale. Institutional operators need to improve data before interpreting national patterns. This is as essential as public charts.
Acceptance criteria. Metrics link to actionable queues; unresolved records have owners and timestamps; public dashboard never reveals reviewer comments or private statuses.
Traceability. [H/N].
INS-005 — Institution comparison and equity context  [P1]
Requirement. Permit comparing institution or governorate activity across periods, normalized by explicitly sourced denominator if available (e.g., registered contributing institutions). Show absolute values next to normalized rates and missing denominator warnings.
UX / decision rationale. Large institutions dominate raw volume. A fair comparison requires context and strong caveats.
Acceptance criteria. Users can see numerator/denominator and sources; missing baselines disable rate metrics; comparisons never label low-coverage entities “underperforming.”
Traceability. [N].
INS-006 — Decision annotation and export  [P1]
Requirement. Allow authorized analysts to save a scoped dashboard snapshot with interpretations, confidence/limitations notes, a last-refresh value, and export for review.
UX / decision rationale. Decisions must be auditable and reproducible rather than screenshots with unknown filters.
Acceptance criteria. Saved insight references dataset version, metric definition, query and author; refreshing does not silently rewrite previously saved conclusions.
Traceability. [N].
10. Record creation, ingestion and editorial workflow
OPS-001 — Structured record editor  [P0]
Requirement. Editor creates/updates research in grouped steps: identity and type; contributors; institutions and location; taxonomy; abstract, keywords and source; review. Required fields and constraints change by research type. Autosave server-side drafts, resume and show errors inline.
UX / decision rationale. The HTML form groups basics/researchers/academic location/content; production needs recoverability, dynamic validation and multi-institution records.
Acceptance criteria. Required fields are enforced server-side; nested hierarchy is valid; concurrent edit shows conflict; draft is recoverable; invalid research cannot publish via direct API.
Traceability. [H/N].
OPS-002 — Staged CSV/Excel import  [P0]
Requirement. Preserve the four-step concept: file upload → column mapping → validation → review/commit. Add secure parsing, async processing, row-level results, dry run, duplicate matching, known reference normalization, batch rollback policy and audit.
UX / decision rationale. Preview-before-commit prevents uncontrolled ingestion, especially when incoming institutions use heterogeneous schemas.
Acceptance criteria. No row is persisted before explicit commit; error rows are never silently dropped; partial-import policy is visible; batch status can be resumed; import records actor, source file and transformed values; template is downloadable.
Traceability. [H/N].
OPS-003 — Governed publication states  [P0]
Requirement. Support states draft → entered → review → approved → published, plus rejected with required reason, returned-for-changes and archived/superseded if approved. Editors submit; reviewers assess; only authorized reviewers/admins publish. Require independent review policy where applicable.
UX / decision rationale. Existence in the catalog is not automatic institutional verification or scholarly accreditation; state transitions must be explicit and accountable.
Acceptance criteria. Each transition checks allowed origin/target, required fields and actor permissions; rejection requires reason; public endpoint includes only published; every change is immutable-audited.
Traceability. [H/P/N].
OPS-004 — Quality checks and duplicate review  [P0]
Requirement. Flag missing fields, invalid geographic hierarchy, unrecognized organization, implausible year, source problems, suspicious duplicates and researcher ambiguity. Suggestions must be reversible, with human resolution and documented criteria.
UX / decision rationale. The prototype’s required-field score and 85% title similarity are starting heuristics—not definitive duplicate detection.
Acceptance criteria. A reviewer can distinguish errors vs warnings, inspect matched evidence, merge/link/mark-not-duplicate with audit; no auto-deletion/auto-rejection by an AI model.
Traceability. [H/N].
OPS-005 — Taxonomy and reference-data administration  [P0]
Requirement. Admin manages governorates/cities, organization types and hierarchies, institutions, academic disciplines, branches, research types, keyword synonyms, aliases and configurable custom fields. Stable IDs and history survive renames.
UX / decision rationale. Free-text universities in the prototype would create fragmented aggregates; shared vocabularies underpin every filter, map and dashboard.
Acceptance criteria. Aliases map to canonical entities, historical records remain addressable, changes trigger controlled reindexing and impact preview; admins cannot break required schema via custom fields.
Traceability. [H/N].
OPS-006 — Auditability, correction and access  [P0]
Requirement. Store every creation, update, status transition, bulk import, taxonomy change and permission change in a server-side append-only audit log with actor, timestamp, before/after, reason and request ID. Expose a public correction channel.
UX / decision rationale. Browser-local audit from the prototype is not trustworthy in production; academic provenance requires accountability.
Acceptance criteria. Audit cannot be edited via app UI; export follows authorization; public correction creates ticket, never silently edits a published record.
Traceability. [H/N].

11.2 Required data constraints
Identity: use UUID/ULID or database IDs internally. Preserve the prototype’s human-readable pattern `SRP-{YYYY}-{GOV}-{######}` as a display/reference identifier only after collision-safe backend generation; support external persistent IDs where verified.
Many-to-many: one record may belong to multiple institutions and contributors; record source ownership is distinct from intellectual authorship.
Research location: distinguish institution registered site, author affiliation, research execution site, study subject geography and map aggregation policy.
Coverage and completeness: a reporting institution may confirm zero submissions, may not have reported, or may be out of scope; these cases must not collapse into one `0`.
Source and rights: metadata rights, document file license, access type, DOI/URL status, embargo and withdrawal must be explicit.
Temporal model: distinguish publication year, submission date, review date, research period, import timestamp and source last-verified date.
Provenance: retain original file, external URL or institutional declaration, import batch, reviewer evidence and record revision.
Institution governance: universities, hospitals, centers, labs, academic institutes and other types share a canonical organization taxonomy, not separate hard-coded single-value fields.
11.3 Source status and opportunity semantics
Use distinct status vocabulary: record review status (draft/entered/review/approved/published/rejected), source availability (full text/abstract only/metadata only/restricted/broken), organization verification (verified/unverified/disputed), reporting coverage (complete/partial/missing/unknown), and gap inference (observed low count/qualified potential opportunity/insufficient evidence). Do not substitute one status for another.

13. UX system, rationale and interface specifications
13.1 Visual hierarchy and shell
Design direction: credible academic/public-infrastructure aesthetic; restrained navy + teal, gold only for meaningful emphasis, soft neutral backgrounds, ample whitespace. Prototype palette is a reference, not a binding visual identity.
Desktop layout: on 1440px, global header; slim primary nav; shared context/filter bar; content canvas; optional collapsible left/right inspector respecting RTL. Default workspaces are separate routes, not a wall of all widgets.
Density: reduce visual clutter by prioritizing one primary CTA per surface and progressively disclosing analytical controls. Avoid decorative badges, emoji-dependent labels and oversized heading bars.
Typography: use an Arabic typeface approved for legibility and platform availability with robust Latin pairing; test Arabic shaping and diacritics in tables and charts. Use semantic sizes (H1/H2/body/caption) and consistent 8px spacing tokens.
Interactions: hover never exclusively reveals important information; use contextual panel, keyboard focus and tap targets. Map, table and chart selection share clear selected states and a single undo/clear path.
Responsive: desktop ≥1200 with map and side panels; tablet 768–1199 with collapsible rail; mobile ≤767 with one-pane layout, map list alternative, bottom-sheet details and independent dashboard charts.
Color: no red-only “gap” states; show text labels, patterns/icons and confidence. Design color tokens in both light and dark theme, keeping contrast and print/export behavior.

13.3 Core user flows (must be prototyped)
Research discovery: enter topic → inspect suggested interpretation → apply facets → inspect results/map → open detail → check original source → compare or export.
Geographic exploration: choose metric → choose governorate → inspect institutions → switch sector/subject → inspect evidence and coverage → open matching studies.
Decision insight: choose period and cohort → review KPI definitions → explore top distributions → open gap evidence → save/export a qualified insight.

14. Accessibility, language and inclusion
Accessibility target: WCAG 2.2 AA design and testing target, including keyboard order, landmarks, modal focus management, error summaries, visible focus, contrast, scalable text and adequate target size. Verify across browsers rather than claiming compliance by default.
Map alternatives: governorate list, data table, keyboard map region selection, legends readable without color, tooltips reachable by focus; assistive tech should not be forced into pan/zoom.
Arabic-first: correct RTL navigation, filters, pagination and modals; LTR isolation for DOIs, URLs, email and English titles; Arabic/Western numeral option by locale; no mirroring of charts where time direction would be misleading.
Content localization: fields may have Arabic, English, both or unknown; never machine-translate official titles without labeling them as suggested, not verified translations.
Network and device inclusivity: low-bandwidth mode disables expensive tiles/animation, prioritizes textual data and works on mid-range mobile devices; retain core discovery without map imagery.

17. Analytics definitions and research-gap methodology
17.1 Metric definitions
Metric
Definition / caveat
Published research records
Distinct published, public-scope research IDs, deduplicated by canonical ID; always show scope and last refresh.
Contributing institutions
Distinct verified organizations linked to in-scope records; separate active reporting institutions from institutions with historical records.
Active governorates
Distinct governorates with verified record affiliation/collection geography for chosen metric; specify mapping rule.
Researcher count
Deduplicated verified researcher IDs, not raw author-name strings; if disambiguation incomplete, label as estimated contributors.
Metadata completeness
Weighted or unweighted required-field presence with a published formula by research type; never substitute for scientific quality.
Coverage ratio
Reporting organizations verified / organizations expected to report in a defined cohort and period; denominator comes from an approved inventory.
Duplicate candidate rate
Unresolved potential duplicate pairs divided by eligible records, not automatically confirmed duplication.
Time to publish
Time from submission to public publication for completed workflow records, segmented by institution/period.

17.2 Rules for “gap” and “opportunity” labels
Choose the analytical unit: governorate × research topic × time window × organization sector and clarify whether measures use institution affiliation or fieldwork location.
Calculate observed count from published and provenance-verified records; display reporting completeness and missingness before interpreting the count.
Use a predefined eligibility threshold for claiming a comparison is interpretable; until agreed, display “Insufficient coverage” instead of an opportunity score.
Compare with approved baselines such as history or reporting-adjusted peers; label methodological assumptions, confidence and normalization.
Show candidate opportunity only when coverage is sufficient and an approved comparison identifies a disparity. This flag is a prompt for expert investigation, not a claim of national research need.
Store the scoring/version logic and enable drill-down to underlying records and missing-reporting organizations. Allow human annotation and methodological disputes.
Illustrative signal, not a product fact: a governorate with 3 indexed records and only 10% expected institutional reporting cannot be ranked below another with 15 records and 95% reporting as a proven “research gap.” The first has a coverage gap; it may or may not have a research-production gap.
18. Validation, testing, success and rollout
18.1 Definition of done and critical test scenarios
Permission safety: API queries, details, map counts, autocomplete, exports and dashboards return no private/unpublished data to public readers; changing client role selector cannot escalate access.
Sync consistency: a governorate filter from map yields the same scoped count, list and dashboard aggregate; reloading a deep link reproduces state; clearing filters restores baseline.
Institutional scope: an editor cannot alter another organization’s record or upload into an unassigned institution; multi-affiliated records follow an approved ownership policy.
Coverage honesty: zero/no coverage/unknown are distinct UI and API states; map and dashboard do not call an unqualified zero a “gap.”
Review policy: unauthorized state transition fails; approval records actor/time/reason; published-only content is public; rejection has reason; editor sees corrections.
Import integrity: invalid rows stay uncommitted; rerunning an idempotent batch does not duplicate records; taxonomy alias and row warnings are traceable.
Search explainability: parsed filters and suggestion explanations correspond to actual query execution; Arabic normalization does not remove meaningful field distinctions.
Accessibility: all top tasks executable by keyboard and screen reader, responsive map table alternative, chart tables, Arabic/English and visible focus.
Robustness: performance tests with large data; resilience to malformed CSV/XLSX, broken URLs, multiple contributors, missing coordinates, simultaneous edits and dashboard stale caches.
18.2 Proposed product success metrics (targets to baseline)
Discovery: median time to first relevant record, search-to-detail success, zero-result rate, relevant-source opening rate, compare usage, task success by researcher cohort.
Contribution: submission completion, import row success after review, median review turnaround, rejection/fix cycle, percentage of records with verified source.
Reliability and trust: published records with complete provenance, duplicate resolution time, institutional reporting coverage, audited critical changes, dashboard trust/comprehension study.
Decision support: share of insights with visible scope and confidence, number of qualified gap investigations completed, evidence export use, reduction in incorrect “zero means none” interpretations.
Measurement rule: propose numeric adoption/quality goals only after pilot baseline with Syrian institutions. No fabricated uplift percentage or invented national coverage target belongs in the first PRD.
18.3 Rollout phases
Discovery and policy alignment: validate organization taxonomy, ownership, publication/verification authorities, data agreements, baseline inventory, scope and ethics with founding stakeholders.
P0 pilot: representative mix of university, hospital and research center; manual curation of test records; public catalog, simple map, governance, imports and quality KPIs.
P1 analytical release: test institution profiles, comparison, thematic and collaboration layers, denominator-aware gap matrix and explainable search with research users and analysts.
National expansion: onboarding, source integration, reporting agreements, data quality SLAs, training, third-party security/accessibility tests and ongoing public methodology updates.
